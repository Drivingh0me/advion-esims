\subsection{Replenishing solvent}
The typical solvent used for the ``mobile phase'' of the ESI-MS is a mixture of methanol, water, and formic acid. 
Each of these should be HPLC grade to prevent particles from stopping up the PEEK tubing. 
This mixture can damage the solvent intake filter that is immersed into the solvent resivior.
Check the polymer filter for damage or brittleness to make sure it is still in good condition.
If the filter is in poor condition, it will leach plastic particles into the isocratic pump, causing it to fail (this has happened previously).

\section{Maintenance Calendar}
The periodic maintenance of the instrument is paramount for sustaining reliable usage and preventing damage.
The following is a list of all the periodic maintenance listed from most frequent to least.

I recommend calibration every Monday morning.

\item \textbf{Cleaning (1 - 3 months)}\\
This is more as needed.

\item \textbf{Pump oil change (1 year)}\\
I recommend changing the oil every January.


\end{itemize}


\section{What NOT to Do}
There are multiple small things that can either damage the instrument or suppress signal.
This is a list of things to avoid when using or maintaining the ESI-MS.
Items listed in \textcolor{red}{red} will damage the instrument and should never be done.
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{\textcolor{red}{Halogenated solvents}}\\
	Halogenated solvents like chloroform and DCM damage the PEEK tubing that carries the solvent through the instrument.
	
	\item \textbf{\textcolor{red}{No nitrogen flow}}\\
	
	\item \textbf{\textcolor{red}{Pulling on isocratic pump}}\\
	
	\item \textbf{\textcolor{red}{Shaking the instrument}}\\
	
	\item \textbf{\textcolor{red}{Not fully dissolved samples}}\\
	
	\item \textbf{Using non-HPLC grade solvent}\\
	
	\item \textbf{Non-volatile solvents}\\
	Do not use high boiling point solvents like DMSO, DMF, or NMP for sample preparation or as the mobile solution.

\end{itemize}


\section{FAQ}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Why is the tune not working for a single mass?}\\
		Sometimes a fragment mass for the runing solution is not bright on the tuning run.
		Remove the mass from the calibration proceedure for preliminary calibration and return the mass at a later calibration.
	\item \textbf{How often should I tune the instrument?}\\
		Once every 7 or 14 days is typically good practice.
		
	\item \textbf{Why are my sample peaks negative on the counts/time graph?}\\
		Not sure, but I have occasionally resolved this issue by simply diluting the sample more.
		It does appear that this sometimes is just a characteristic of a poor analyte that may not be studied well by ESI-MS.
\end{itemize}
